# Questions to Ask a BHRT Clinic Before You Start

You've done the research. You've read about bioidentical hormone replacement therapy, recognized your own symptoms in every article, and finally decided to make an appointment — and now a different kind of anxiety has set in. The questions to ask a BHRT clinic before you commit to treatment are just as important as the decision to explore BHRT in the first place. The clinic you choose will shape your entire experience: how carefully you're evaluated, how thoughtfully your dose is adjusted, and whether you finally get the relief you've been looking for — or end up frustrated and starting over.

This guide gives you a complete, plain-language BHRT consultation questions list you can take into any hormone clinic appointment. These aren't hypothetical questions — they're the ones that reveal whether a provider is genuinely equipped to manage your care or just running patients through a script.

## Why the Right Questions to Ask a BHRT Clinic Actually Matter

Asking the right questions to ask a BHRT clinic protects you from two opposite failure modes: undertreated care and reckless prescribing. The BHRT landscape is genuinely varied. On one end, you'll find thoughtful, evidence-informed clinicians who treat the whole person and adjust carefully over time. On the other end, you'll find high-volume "wellness" operations that hand out the same pellet protocol to everyone who walks through the door and call it personalized medicine.

The problem is that both types of clinics look similar from the outside. They use the same language, the same testimonials, and sometimes the same marketing. The difference only becomes visible when you start asking specific, pointed questions — and when you pay attention to how the clinic responds.

A provider who welcomes your questions, answers them without defensiveness, and uses language like "it depends on your labs" and "we'll monitor and adjust" is showing you something real. A provider who gives vague non-answers, pivots to testimonials, or seems impatient with your concerns is also showing you something real.

If you haven't yet figured out what credentials and background to look for before you even get to the consultation, our guide to [what to look for in a BHRT provider](/what-to-look-for-in-a-bhrt-provider/) is a strong starting point.

## Questions About Provider Credentials and Experience

Your first set of questions should establish who, exactly, will be overseeing your hormone therapy — and whether they have the training to do it well.

**Ask:**
- What is your medical background and licensing? (MD, DO, NP, PA?)
- How long have you been prescribing BHRT specifically?
- Do you specialize in hormone health, or is this one part of a broader functional or wellness practice?
- Who oversees care if you're not available?

A licensed prescriber with dedicated hormone therapy experience is non-negotiable. This doesn't mean you need an endocrinologist — many skilled BHRT providers are OB-GYNs, internists, or well-trained nurse practitioners. But it does mean the person managing your care should be able to explain the mechanisms of what they're prescribing in plain language, field your concerns with confidence, and escalate appropriately when something falls outside their scope.

Be wary of clinics where the prescriber is difficult to identify, where you'll be "managed" primarily by administrative staff, or where the business model seems designed to minimize physician time. The dose adjustment period — which many patients describe as their biggest frustration — requires a real clinician relationship, not a patient portal message that takes five days to get a generic reply.

## Questions About Testing and Lab Work

A reputable BHRT clinic will not prescribe anything before running comprehensive baseline bloodwork. This is non-negotiable, and how a clinic answers questions about labs tells you a great deal about how seriously they take individualized care.

**Ask:**
- What labs do you run before prescribing?
- Do you test free and total hormone levels, not just total?
- How do you interpret results — against standard lab reference ranges, or against symptom-optimized ranges?
- How often will you retest after starting treatment?
- Does the cost of follow-up labs come with my program, or is it billed separately?

At minimum, pre-treatment labs should include estradiol, progesterone, total and free testosterone, DHEA-S, SHBG (sex hormone-binding globulin), thyroid panel, and cortisol. Many thoughtful providers also include a complete metabolic panel, insulin levels, and cardiovascular markers.

The distinction between "total" and "free" hormone levels matters. Free testosterone, for example, reflects the hormone actually available to your tissues — total testosterone alone can be misleading. A clinic that only runs total hormone panels may be missing the clinical picture.

Also pay attention to how the clinic interprets results. Standard reference ranges are built for population averages, not for symptom resolution. Some providers use functional or optimal ranges that better correlate with how patients actually feel. This isn't fringe medicine — it's a recognized clinical consideration, and it's worth discussing openly.

## Questions About Delivery Methods and Individualization

BHRT comes in several delivery formats — creams, gels, patches, oral capsules, sublingual troches, and pellets — and no single format is right for everyone. How a clinic answers questions about delivery methods signals whether they're taking a truly individualized approach or defaulting to whatever they happen to sell.

**Ask:**
- What delivery methods do you offer?
- How do you decide which delivery method is right for a specific patient?
- Do you use compounded hormones, FDA-approved bioidentical products, or both?
- If pellets are your primary method, what happens if my dose needs to be adjusted between insertion appointments?

Some clinics are, essentially, pellet-only practices. This isn't inherently wrong, but it does limit options. Pellets can't be removed once inserted, which means that if your dose is wrong — too high or too low — you're waiting three to six months for correction. For patients who are new to hormone therapy and still finding their optimal dose, this can be a real problem.

The NAMS (North American Menopause Society) acknowledges that while some bioidentical hormones are FDA-approved, compounded preparations carry less regulatory oversight, and patients should be informed of this distinction. A good clinic will explain this clearly rather than glossing over it.

## Questions About Monitoring, Dose Adjustment, and Ongoing Support

The dose adjustment period is the phase most patients say they weren't prepared for — and it's where the quality of a clinic becomes most visible. Many people feel meaningfully better within the first few weeks, but finding the optimal dose often takes two to four months of gradual adjustment. A clinic that doesn't have a clear, responsive protocol for this phase will leave you stranded.

**Ask:**
- What does the monitoring process look like after I start treatment?
- How do I report symptoms between appointments?
- How quickly can my dose be adjusted if something isn't working?
- What is your protocol if I experience side effects?
- What's the typical timeline to find my optimal dose?

Expect an honest answer that normalizes the adjustment process rather than promising immediate, perfect results. The clinics that over-promise on timelines are often the same ones that under-deliver on support.

Also ask about communication channels. Can you message your provider directly? Is there a nurse you can call if something feels off? What's the turnaround time for non-urgent questions? These logistics matter enormously when you're in the middle of a dosing adjustment and not sure whether what you're feeling is normal.

## Quick-Reference: BHRT Consultation Questions Checklist

Use this before-starting-BHRT checklist at your appointment. Print it, take notes, and don't leave without answers you actually understand.

| Category | Questions to Ask |
|---|---|
| **Credentials** | License type? Years of BHRT experience? Who covers care in your absence? |
| **Labs** | Which tests run before prescribing? Free + total levels? How often retested? |
| **Delivery methods** | What options do you offer? How is method selected? Compounded or FDA-approved? |
| **Dosing philosophy** | Individualized or protocol-driven? How are doses adjusted? |
| **Monitoring** | How do I report symptoms? Turnaround time for adjustments? |
| **Cost transparency** | Total program cost? What's included? Are follow-up labs extra? |
| **Red flags to watch** | Skipping labs, no monitoring plan, pushing one method for everyone, vague answers |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I ask a BHRT clinic before starting treatment?

Ask about the provider's credentials and experience with hormone therapy, which labs they run before prescribing, which delivery methods they offer, how they monitor and adjust dosing, what the total cost is including follow-up labs, and whether they take an individualized or protocol-driven approach. These questions help you evaluate whether the clinic is genuinely equipped to manage your care safely and responsively.

### How do I know if a BHRT clinic is legitimate?

A legitimate BHRT clinic will require comprehensive baseline labs before prescribing anything, have licensed prescribers on staff (MD, DO, NP, or PA), explain their monitoring protocol clearly, and never guarantee results or pressure you to commit before evaluation. Red flags include clinics that skip labs, push a single product aggressively, or can't explain the reasoning behind their dosing decisions.

### What labs should a BHRT clinic run before prescribing?

At minimum, a reputable BHRT clinic should test estradiol, progesterone, total and free testosterone, DHEA-S, thyroid panel (TSH, free T3, free T4), and cortisol. Many providers also test sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG), insulin, and a complete metabolic panel. Skipping baseline labs is a significant red flag — there is no safe way to dose hormones without knowing where your levels actually start.

### Is it normal to feel nervous before a BHRT consultation?

Completely normal — health anxiety before a first BHRT appointment is nearly universal. Many patients have spent years being dismissed by doctors, so walking into a new clinic requires real courage. The best way to manage pre-appointment anxiety is to arrive prepared with a written symptom history and a list of questions, so you feel like an active participant in the conversation rather than someone waiting to be told what to do.

## Common Questions About Choosing a BHRT Clinic

### How do I prepare for my first BHRT consultation?

Before your appointment, write down every symptom you're experiencing, how long you've had it, and how much it's affecting your daily life. Bring any recent lab work, a list of current medications and supplements, and your written list of questions. Coming prepared helps you use the time effectively and gives your provider a clearer clinical picture from the start.

### Can I switch BHRT clinics if I'm not happy with my current one?

Yes, and you don't need your current clinic's permission to do so. Request copies of your lab results and treatment history — you're legally entitled to your records — and bring them to your new consultation. A good new provider will review your history before making any changes to your regimen rather than starting from scratch unnecessarily.

### What's the difference between a BHRT clinic and an endocrinologist?

Endocrinologists specialize in the entire endocrine system and are primarily trained to manage diagnosable conditions like diabetes, thyroid disease, and adrenal disorders. BHRT clinics — often staffed by OB-GYNs, internists, or functional medicine practitioners — tend to focus on age-related hormone decline and symptom optimization. Neither is automatically superior; what matters most is the individual provider's experience with the specific type of hormone therapy you're seeking.

### How long does a typical BHRT consultation take?

Most initial BHRT consultations run 45 to 90 minutes, depending on the clinic. The first appointment typically includes a detailed symptom and health history intake, review of any existing labs, a physical assessment, and a discussion of potential treatment approaches. Be cautious of clinics where the initial consultation is very brief — a thorough intake is a sign of individualized care.

### Do BHRT clinics accept insurance?

Most BHRT clinics operate on a cash-pay or membership model, particularly those using compounded hormones, which are not covered by most insurance plans. Some FDA-approved bioidentical products (like certain estradiol patches and progesterone capsules) may be covered depending on your plan. Always ask about the full cost structure — including labs, follow-up appointments, and the hormones themselves — before committing to a program.
