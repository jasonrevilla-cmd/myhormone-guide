# What Happens at Your First BHRT Consultation — And How to Walk In Ready

If you have spent months researching symptoms, reading studies, and finally worked up the courage to book a first BHRT consultation, the last thing you need is to show up and feel blindsided. Many people arrive at their first hormone consultation anxious, unsure what questions to ask, and worried they will be dismissed the same way they have been by other providers. That anxiety is completely valid — and completely unnecessary once you know what to expect.

This guide walks you through the entire process from start to finish: what happens before you arrive, what the appointment actually looks like, what labs are typically ordered, and what realistic timelines look like for getting answers and starting treatment. By the end, you will feel ready to walk in calm, prepared, and in control of the conversation.

## What to Expect Before Your First BHRT Consultation

Before your appointment even begins, most BHRT clinics will send you intake paperwork — and the depth of that paperwork is itself a quality signal. Expect to fill out a detailed symptom questionnaire that covers far more than hot flashes and night sweats. Comprehensive intake forms typically ask about:

- **Sleep quality and patterns** — how long it takes you to fall asleep, whether you wake at night, how rested you feel
- **Cognitive function** — brain fog, memory lapses, difficulty concentrating
- **Mood and mental health** — anxiety, depression, irritability, emotional flatness
- **Libido and sexual function** — changes in desire, arousal, or comfort
- **Weight and metabolism** — unexplained weight gain, changes in body composition despite diet or exercise
- **Energy and fatigue** — not just tiredness, but the bone-deep exhaustion many perimenopausal women describe
- **Menstrual cycle history** — for women, this is crucial diagnostic context

Some clinics also ask about your cardiovascular history, bone density, and family history of hormone-sensitive cancers. Answer everything honestly and thoroughly. The more accurate picture your provider has before you walk in, the more productive the appointment will be.

If you have any existing lab results — a recent thyroid panel, lipid panel, or previous hormone levels from a primary care visit — bring those with you. Even if they are "normal" by conventional standards, a BHRT-trained provider may interpret them differently.

## How the First BHRT Appointment Actually Unfolds

Your first BHRT appointment is primarily a conversation, not a procedure. Think of it less like a typical rushed doctor's visit and more like a structured intake interview where you are expected to participate actively.

Most consultations open with the provider reviewing your intake forms and asking follow-up questions. A good clinician will ask clarifying questions that help them build a timeline — when did symptoms start, what has changed, what have you already tried. This part of the visit can feel surprisingly emotional for people who have spent years being told their symptoms are "just stress" or "part of aging." Many patients report that simply being believed and taken seriously is itself a meaningful part of the first appointment.

After the symptom review, your provider will typically:

1. **Take a health history** — reviewing conditions, medications, supplements, surgical history, and family history
2. **Discuss your health goals** — not just symptom relief, but long-term objectives like bone health, cardiovascular protection, cognitive function, and quality of life
3. **Conduct or order a physical assessment** — this may include blood pressure, weight, and depending on the clinic, a brief physical exam
4. **Explain the testing process** — what labs they plan to order, why, and what the results will reveal

One thing that separates high-quality hormone providers from assembly-line clinics: they spend time explaining why they are ordering specific tests rather than just sending you to the lab with a generic panel. If your provider cannot explain the rationale for each test they order, that is worth noting. Our guide to [hormone testing for women: what tests to ask for](/hormone-testing-for-women-what-tests-to-ask-for/) explains exactly which labs matter and why — worth reading before your appointment so you can ask informed questions.

## What Lab Work Is Typically Ordered After a BHRT Intake Appointment

Comprehensive baseline lab work is non-negotiable before starting any hormone therapy. A responsible BHRT provider will not prescribe based on symptoms alone — they need objective data to establish your baseline and personalize your protocol.

For women, a thorough hormone panel typically includes:

| Lab Test | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| Estradiol (E2) | Primary estrogen; often low or erratic in perimenopause |
| Progesterone | Frequently deficient even when estrogen is "normal" |
| Testosterone (total and free) | Often overlooked in women; affects energy, libido, and cognition |
| FSH and LH | Pituitary hormones that signal where you are in the menopause transition |
| DHEA-S | Adrenal androgen; supports energy and immune function |
| Thyroid panel (TSH, Free T3, Free T4) | Thyroid dysfunction mimics and compounds hormone imbalance |
| Fasting insulin and glucose | Metabolic context for treatment planning |
| Complete blood count and metabolic panel | Safety baseline before treatment begins |

For men, the panel shifts to include total and free testosterone, estradiol, PSA (prostate-specific antigen), hematocrit, and LH/FSH to assess whether low testosterone is primary or secondary.

Some providers also test cortisol, sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG), and vitamin D — all of which can meaningfully influence how hormones are metabolized and how well treatment works.

Results typically take three to seven business days. A reputable clinic will schedule a dedicated follow-up appointment to review your results in detail — not deliver them through a patient portal note with no context.

## What Gets Decided at the Follow-Up Appointment

The follow-up visit — typically one to three weeks after your intake appointment — is where the real treatment conversation happens. Your provider will walk through your lab results in the context of your symptoms, your health history, and your goals.

This is the appointment where delivery method, dosing, and monitoring frequency get discussed. BHRT can be delivered as topical creams or gels, oral capsules, patches, injections, vaginal preparations, or subcutaneous pellets — and each has a different absorption profile, risk-benefit consideration, and lifestyle fit. A good provider will explain the options and their reasoning rather than defaulting to a single modality for every patient.

This is also the time to be honest about your concerns. Many people arrive at this appointment carrying worry about cancer risk (often rooted in the now-significantly-revised interpretation of the 2002 Women's Health Initiative study), fear of feeling "different" on hormones, or anxiety about the dose adjustment period. A knowledgeable provider will address these directly — and if they dismiss your concerns rather than engaging with them, that is important information about whether this is the right fit.

Before this appointment, it is worth reviewing the [questions to ask a BHRT clinic before you start](/questions-to-ask-a-bhrt-clinic-before-you-start/) so you arrive prepared to evaluate the answers you are getting, not just absorb information passively.

## Red Flags and Green Flags: How to Read the Quality of a BHRT Consultation

Not all hormone clinics are created equal. The consultation experience itself tells you a great deal about the quality of care you are likely to receive.

**Green flags — signs you are in good hands:**

- The intake process is thorough and the forms ask about your whole health picture, not just hormones
- Your provider listens more than they talk, especially in the first visit
- Labs are ordered before any prescription is written
- The provider explains *why* they are recommending a specific delivery method or dose
- They discuss monitoring — follow-up labs, re-evaluation timelines, and what to do if you have concerns between appointments
- They acknowledge your previous experiences with conventional medicine without dismissing those providers

**Red flags — signs to proceed with caution or look elsewhere:**

- Prescriptions offered at the first visit, before lab results
- A one-size-fits-all protocol with no discussion of your individual history
- Dismissal of safety questions about cancer risk or cardiovascular effects
- No clear plan for follow-up monitoring
- High-pressure sales tactics or packages that must be purchased upfront
- A provider who cannot clearly explain the difference between synthetic and bioidentical hormones

Knowing what good looks like makes it much easier to find it. Our detailed guide to [what to look for in a BHRT provider](/what-to-look-for-in-a-bhrt-provider/) goes deeper on how to evaluate credentials, clinical approach, and red flags specific to hormone clinics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a first BHRT consultation typically take?

Most first BHRT consultations run between 45 and 90 minutes. The extra time is intentional — a thorough provider needs to review your full symptom history, lifestyle factors, and health goals before ordering labs or discussing treatment options. If a clinic is rushing you through in 20 minutes, that is a warning sign worth heeding.

### Do I need bloodwork before my first BHRT appointment?

It depends on the clinic. Some providers require baseline lab work before your first visit so they can review results during the consultation. Others will conduct the intake appointment first, then order labs based on your symptom profile. Either approach is valid — what matters is that comprehensive hormone testing happens before any treatment is prescribed.

### What should I bring to my first hormone consultation?

Bring a list of your current medications and supplements, any recent lab results you have access to, a written summary of your symptoms (including when they started and how they affect your daily life), and a list of questions for your provider. Coming prepared helps you use the appointment time efficiently and signals to the provider that you are a serious, engaged patient.

### Will I leave my first BHRT appointment with a prescription?

Rarely, and you should be cautious if a clinic tries to prescribe on the first visit without lab results. Most responsible providers use the first appointment for intake and symptom review, order baseline bloodwork, and then schedule a follow-up to discuss a personalized treatment plan once results are in. Good hormone care is not one-size-fits-all or instant.

## Common Questions About Your First BHRT Consultation

### What actually happens at a BHRT consultation?

A first BHRT consultation typically involves a detailed symptom review, a full health and medication history, a discussion of your health goals, and the ordering of baseline hormone labs. Most appointments last 45 to 90 minutes. You will not usually leave with a prescription — responsible providers wait for lab results before recommending any treatment.

### How do I prepare for a hormone consultation?

Write down your symptoms and when they started, list every medication and supplement you take, gather any recent lab results you already have, and prepare a few questions for your provider. The more organized you arrive, the more productive the appointment will be — and the faster you will move toward a treatment plan.

### Is a BHRT consultation covered by insurance?

Coverage varies widely. Some providers operate on a cash-pay or membership model, while others bill insurance for the consultation and lab work separately. Call your insurance company before the appointment and ask whether hormone consultations and the specific lab codes your provider plans to order are covered under your plan.

### How soon after my first consultation will I start BHRT?

Most patients start BHRT two to four weeks after their initial consultation — after labs are completed, results are reviewed at a follow-up appointment, and a personalized treatment plan is agreed upon. Clinics that promise same-day treatment should be approached with caution.

### Can I see a telehealth provider for my first BHRT appointment?

Yes. Many qualified BHRT providers now offer telehealth consultations, which can be a practical option if you live in an area with limited access to hormone specialists. Lab work can typically be ordered to a local draw site. Just apply the same quality criteria you would use for an in-person provider — credentials, thoroughness, and a clear lab-before-prescription policy all still apply.
